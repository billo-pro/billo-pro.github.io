Boss Team Preparation

Gather and prepare the correct Pokémon to defeat any boss in the game!
Introduction

Boss fights are a crucial part of the PvE end-game, offering players an additional challenge alongside valuable rewards. Defeating a boss rewards players with two forms of in-game currency (Poké Dollars and PvE Coins) as well as rare and, in some cases, exclusive Pokémon. Achieving a three-win streak against any boss also gives players the opportunity to choose from a selection of Pokémon that can either be syncronized or have increased shiny rates.

This page will focus on providing insight on which teams are effective against each boss, cover the locations of which you can find the recommended Pokémon, and why certain compositions are preferred against specific bosses in an easily to digest format.

Common Team Compositions

Understand boss team structures and where to hunt for the Pokémon you need to build your team!
Main Team Overview:

The team compositions below are effective for most bosses in the game. Both teams are fairly similar, with the main difference being our lead Pokémon. The lead Pokémon should change depending on if the first Pokémon the boss uses is considered to be problematic or not. Examples of "problematic" Pokémon include Mega Evolutions, {% include tooltips/roar.html %}/{% include tooltips/whirlwind.html %} users, and certain abilities like {% include tooltips/no-guard.html %}.

When the boss has a problematic lead, we will lead with Mega Banette, otherwise we will lead with Whimsicott. Both have the ability {% include tooltips/prankster.html %} and serve a unique purpose at the start of a boss fight, this will be expanded on below. The goal of this team is to successfully debuff the opposing lead, buff our own Pokémon, and ultimately get Pangoro into a position where it can sweep the entire team using a heavily boosted {% include tooltips/power-trip.html %}.

Mega Banette

Mega Banette uses it's {% include tooltips/prankster.html %} ability to allow it to move first when using status moves, provided the defending Pokémon is not a Dark Type. This allows us to reliably use {% include tooltips/destiny-bond.html %} and be knocked out by an opposing problematic Pokémon, safely removing it from the battlefield.

If Mega Banette survives after using {% include tooltips/destiny-bond.html %}, you can continue spamming the move until the opposing Pokémon knocks out Banette. This allows us to bring Whimsicott onto the field without having to deal with the bosses problematic first Pokémon.

Banette Notes
  • Base form Shuppet/Banette do not have the ability Prankster. This means that completing the Mega Bracelet quest and obtaining the Banettite is required. It is recommended to complete these quests before continuing to follow this guide.
  • Banette do not need to have good IVs or a beneficial nature. They only need to have the ability Prankster. Shuppet is most commonly farmed at Mt Pyre (use the in-game Pokédex for more information about spawn locations).
Whimsicott

Whimsicott uses it's {% include tooltips/prankster.html %} ability to allow it to move first when using status moves, provided the defending Pokémon is not a Dark Type. This allows us to reliably use {% include tooltips/switcheroo.html %} to give the defending Pokémon a Lagging Tail, significantly reducing its Speed.

If Whimsicott survives after using Switcheroo, it can then use {% include tooltips/memento.html %} to lower the opposing Pokémon's offensive stats before safely removing itself from the battlefield. This allows us to bring Shuckle onto the field without having to risk a direct switch.

Whimsicott Notes
  • The above set is recommended, but Whimsicott does not necessarily need to be trained. You can use an untrained Cottonee or any other Pokémon with the combination of Prankster + Trick/Switcheroo. Ensure the Pokémon is holding a Lagging Tail.
  • Cottonee/Whimsicott do not need to have good IVs or a beneficial nature. They only need to have the ability Prankster. Cottonee is most commonly farmed at Pinkan Island (use the in-game Pokédex for more information about spawn locations).
Shuckle

Shuckle uses its {% include tooltips/sturdy.html %} ability to guarantee that it survives at least one hit, it can then use the move {% include tooltips/sweet-scent.html %} to harshly lower the evasiveness of the opposing Pokémon, making lower accuracy moves more likely to hit (guaranteed to hit for the purposes of this team).

A trained Shuckle is likely to live more than two attacks due to its naturally high defensive stats, you can use these turns to safely set up moves like {% include tooltips/stealth-rock.html %}, {% include tooltips/sticky-web.html %}, or begin lowering accuracy with {% include tooltips/flash.html %}. Once Shuckle has done it's job it can then be allowed to faint by the opposing Pokémon to provide a safe switch in for Golduck.

Shuckle Notes
  • The above set is recommended, but Shuckle does not necessarily need to be trained. You can use an untrained Shuckle with the ability Sturdy, but having a trained Shuckle is beneficial as it can often live more than one move.
  • Shuckle is most commonly farmed on Route 40 or in the Johto Safari Zone (use the in-game Pokédex for more information about spawn locations).
